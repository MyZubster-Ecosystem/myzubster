# Scientific Study Protocol — Psychoactive and Controlled Substances

## Purpose

This protocol defines a multidisciplinary, public-interest study for MyZubster Life on psychoactive and controlled substances, focusing on public health, social impact, environmental monitoring, prevention, treatment access, and evidence-based policy.

It is **not** a guide to manufacture, obtain, dose, combine, conceal, distribute, or use controlled substances.

## Research questions

1. What are the main health and social burdens associated with major classes of psychoactive substances?
2. Which prevention, harm-reduction, treatment and recovery interventions have the strongest evidence?
3. How do patterns vary over time and across territories without identifying individuals?
4. Can wastewater and other environmental measurements provide useful population-level indicators while preserving privacy?
5. What environmental impacts are associated with pharmaceutical residues, illicit-drug residues, discarded paraphernalia and production waste?
6. Which regulatory and public-health models are associated with measurable changes in mortality, morbidity, treatment access and environmental burden?

## Substance classes in scope

The study may compare broad scientific classes, including:

- opioids;
- stimulants;
- cannabinoids;
- sedatives/hypnotics;
- dissociatives;
- hallucinogens/psychedelics;
- novel psychoactive substances;
- prescription medicines with abuse/dependence potential;
- alcohol and nicotine as comparative legal psychoactive substances where scientifically relevant.

Classification is for epidemiological and scientific analysis only.

## Core outcomes

### Public health

- overdose and poisoning burden;
- emergency-department and hospital utilization;
- mortality and morbidity trends;
- prevalence of substance-use disorders;
- treatment access, retention and recovery outcomes;
- infectious-disease indicators when relevant;
- mental-health comorbidity at population level;
- non-fatal adverse events.

### Social outcomes

- housing instability indicators;
- employment and education disruption;
- barriers to care;
- stigma and discrimination;
- criminal-justice contact at aggregate level;
- community safety indicators.

### Environmental outcomes

- residues/metabolites in wastewater where legally and scientifically appropriate;
- pharmaceutical and drug-related residues in surface water;
- litter and discarded paraphernalia counts using non-identifying methods;
- hazardous waste and contamination linked to illegal production sites where official data exist;
- energy/material footprints of regulated production where published data are available.

## Study design

A phased mixed-methods design is recommended.

### Phase 1 — systematic evidence map

Sources should prioritize:

- WHO;
- UNODC;
- EMCDDA / EUDA;
- European Commission and ECDC where relevant;
- national ministries of health and public-health institutes;
- peer-reviewed systematic reviews and meta-analyses;
- official mortality, hospitalization and wastewater datasets.

Each claim should record source, year, geography, study design, population and evidence quality.

### Phase 2 — territorial ecological analysis

Use aggregate geographic units only. No individual-level profiling or inference from personal email, location history, health records or banking data.

Potential variables:

- population;
- age distribution;
- socioeconomic deprivation indices;
- treatment-service availability;
- overdose mortality;
- emergency presentations;
- aggregate wastewater signals when available;
- environmental litter/waste indicators;
- policy changes and intervention dates.

### Phase 3 — wastewater-based epidemiology pilot

Only with competent scientific/utility partners and legal approval.

The pilot should:

- sample at wastewater-treatment or sewer-catchment level;
- avoid household/building-level inference;
- use validated analytical laboratories;
- document uncertainty, degradation, population normalization and sampling bias;
- publish only sufficiently aggregated results;
- prohibit attempts to identify users or small groups.

### Phase 4 — intervention evaluation

Candidate interventions can include:

- prevention and education;
- naloxone access and overdose prevention;
- evidence-based treatment services;
- mental-health integration;
- recovery and social-support programs;
- safe medication disposal;
- environmental cleanup programs;
- regulatory changes suitable for quasi-experimental evaluation.

## Analysis plan

Where data quality permits:

- interrupted time-series analysis;
- difference-in-differences for policy/intervention comparisons;
- age-standardized rates;
- Bayesian or frequentist uncertainty intervals;
- sensitivity analysis for under-reporting and missing data;
- pre-registered primary and secondary outcomes;
- transparent code and reproducible notebooks using non-sensitive data.

Correlation must not be described as causation without an appropriate design.

## Ethics, privacy and governance

- no publication of identifiable health, criminal, employment or financial information;
- no ingestion of private messages or medical documents for research without specific informed consent and governance approval;
- no household-level or person-level wastewater inference;
- data minimization and purpose limitation;
- ethics/IRB review when human-subjects research applies;
- role-based access to non-public research data;
- retention schedules and deletion procedures;
- documented public-interest purpose;
- community and lived-experience input without exposing participant identity.

## Safety boundaries

The study must not publish actionable instructions for:

- synthesis or extraction of controlled substances;
- cultivation intended to produce illegal drugs;
- dosing, potentiation, concealment or evasion of testing;
- trafficking or unlicensed distribution;
- defeating law-enforcement or medical monitoring.

Medical or treatment claims must be grounded in authoritative clinical evidence and must not replace professional care.

## MyZubster Life integration

Life can represent this research through a non-personal dashboard containing:

- territory-level public-health indicators;
- evidence-quality labels;
- intervention timeline;
- wastewater/environmental indicators;
- treatment-access metrics;
- prevention/harm-reduction program metrics;
- environmental cleanup/reuse metrics;
- research provenance and uncertainty.

Zorgax may summarize evidence, detect missing fields, prepare literature-review tasks and flag contradictory evidence. It must not diagnose individuals or infer drug use from private personal data.

## Initial deliverables

1. Evidence table covering major substance classes.
2. Data dictionary for public-health and environmental indicators.
3. Ethics/privacy checklist.
4. Wastewater pilot protocol template.
5. Intervention-evaluation statistical analysis plan.
6. Public dashboard specification with minimum aggregation thresholds.
7. Reproducible bibliography with source quality grading.

## Success criteria

The study is successful if it produces reproducible, privacy-preserving evidence that helps communities and institutions understand health, social and environmental impacts and evaluate interventions without exposing or profiling individual people.
