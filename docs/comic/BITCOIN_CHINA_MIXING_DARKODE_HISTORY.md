## Narrative map

The comic connects four public-history threads while keeping them distinct:

1. **Bitcoin** — a public blockchain whose transaction history can be analysed even when addresses are pseudonymous.
2. **China** — an important part of Bitcoin's early market history, followed by progressively restrictive policy toward virtual-currency trading and related financial activity.
3. **Mixing services** — services designed to make transaction provenance harder to follow by combining or otherwise obfuscating flows. They have legitimate privacy concepts around them, but multiple services have also been prosecuted or seized in money-laundering cases.
4. **Darkode** — a separate historical cybercrime forum dismantled in 2015. It is included as an external historical reference, not as a MyZubster predecessor or affiliation.

## Evidence cards

### Bitcoin and traceability

Bitcoin addresses are pseudonymous rather than automatically tied to a public real-world identity. However, blockchain analysis can connect transaction patterns and, together with other evidence, identify actors or clusters. The MyZubster narrative uses this as a lesson in the difference between **privacy**, **pseudonymity**, and **provable provenance**.

Sources:
- U.S. DOJ — Bitcoin Fog conviction: https://www.justice.gov/archives/opa/pr/bitcoin-fog-operator-convicted-money-laundering-conspiracy
- Europol — Cryptocurrencies: tracing the evolution of criminal finances: https://www.europol.europa.eu/publications-events/publications/cryptocurrencies-tracing-evolution-of-criminal-finances

### China and virtual currencies

China was significant in Bitcoin's early trading history. Regulatory policy later moved strongly against virtual-currency trading/speculation and related financial activity. The comic treats China as a **regulatory-history chapter**, not as evidence that Bitcoin, mixing, and Darkode were one organisation or one operation.

Source:
- People's Bank of China — policy statement referencing the 2021 Notice on preventing and resolving virtual-currency trading/speculation risks: https://www.pbc.gov.cn/en/3688110/3688172/5552468/2025121116132332435/index.html

### What “mixing” means

At a high level, cryptocurrency mixing attempts to reduce the obvious link between incoming and outgoing funds by pooling, swapping, or otherwise obfuscating transaction flows. This page intentionally stops at the conceptual level. It contains no operational recipe.

Public cases show why the topic belongs in an evidence-first history: Bitcoin Fog operated for years before its operator was convicted and sentenced; ChipMixer was seized in an international law-enforcement action; Europol has documented mixer investigations and seizures.

Sources:
- U.S. DOJ — Bitcoin Fog operator sentenced: https://www.justice.gov/archives/opa/pr/bitcoin-fog-operator-sentenced-money-laundering-conspiracy
- U.S. DOJ — ChipMixer takedown: https://www.justice.gov/archives/opa/pr/justice-department-investigation-leads-takedown-darknet-cryptocurrency-mixer-processed-over-3
- Europol — Bestmixer.io takedown: https://www.europol.europa.eu/media-press/newsroom/news/multi-million-euro-cryptocurrency-laundering-service-bestmixerio-taken-down
- Academic background — *Towards Understanding and Demystifying Bitcoin Mixing Services*: https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.16274

### Darkode

Darkode was a password-protected cybercrime forum. In July 2015 the U.S. Department of Justice announced its dismantling following an FBI infiltration under Operation Shrouded Horizon, as part of coordinated action involving 20 countries.

This historical event is a separate thread from cryptocurrency mixing. The comic connects them only thematically: closed networks, pseudonymity, evidence, investigation, and the limits of assumed anonymity.

Source:
- U.S. DOJ — Major Computer Hacking Forum Dismantled: https://www.justice.gov/usao-wdpa/pr/major-computer-hacking-forum-dismantled
