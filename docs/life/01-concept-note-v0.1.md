## 1. Executive summary

MyZubster-Nature proposes a privacy-aware digital evidence and monitoring workflow for biodiversity and protected-area management. The demonstrator connects field observations, geolocation and media, structured registry records, validation status, evidence records and an institutional dashboard.

The project is intended to demonstrate how a reusable digital workflow can improve the quality, traceability and operational usability of biodiversity observations and monitoring evidence while supporting institutional workflows. The environmental value must be demonstrated through a real pilot, scientifically defined baseline and measurable conservation/monitoring outcomes.

The initial pilot hypothesis is Capraia / Tuscan Archipelago, subject to confirmation by the competent authority. Alternative protected-area pilots will be maintained until an institutional pilot is secured.

## 2. Problem and need

Protected-area and biodiversity work can involve fragmented observations, heterogeneous media, inconsistent metadata, manual validation and weak continuity between field evidence and institutional reporting. A practical system should help convert observations into structured, traceable evidence that can be reviewed and used by competent institutions.

The project therefore focuses on the complete evidence chain rather than on a standalone consumer application:

`observation → GPS/media → registry → validation → evidence → map → institutional dashboard`

## 3. Objectives

1. Demonstrate a reusable digital workflow for biodiversity observations and monitoring evidence.
2. Improve traceability and structured management of field observations.
3. Establish a scientifically defensible baseline and KPI framework with a research partner.
4. Demonstrate the workflow in a real protected-area context.
5. Produce evidence for replication in other protected areas and institutional contexts.

## 4. Proposed solution

The LIFE demonstrator will provide:

- field observation ingestion;
- geolocation and media references;
- structured registry records;
- validation states and provenance metadata;
- evidence records suitable for institutional review;
- map-based exploration;
- a minimal institutional dashboard;
- documented APIs/data structures for future interoperability.

The existing MyZubster platform is treated as a technology base. The LIFE project will implement only the vertical slice necessary to demonstrate the environmental use case.

## 5. Pilot

**Candidate:** Capraia / Tuscan Archipelago.

**Status:** candidate only; no partnership or authorisation is claimed at this stage.

The pilot will be selected on the basis of:

- real biodiversity or protected-area management need;
- competent authority engagement;
- availability/feasibility of baseline data;
- ability to run field monitoring;
- measurable indicators;
- potential for replication.

Two alternative protected-area candidates will be maintained until the first institutional discussion is successful.

## 6. Scientific methodology

No conservation impact numbers are invented in this draft.

## 9. Topic fit — decision point

### NAT-GOV
The project has a strong digital governance/information component because it improves institutional workflows, information quality, stakeholder engagement and the usability of biodiversity evidence. However, it must not be framed as a generic IT platform: the environmental objective and measurable biodiversity benefit remain central.

### NAT-NATURE
NAT-NATURE is potentially stronger if the project is designed around a concrete improvement in species/habitat condition, area-based conservation/restoration, or another eligible nature action. A purely digital monitoring platform would not be enough. The project would need a clear conservation intervention and measurable conservation benefit.

**Current working assumption:** discuss NAT-GOV with the NCP while explicitly asking whether the proposed pilot and conservation outcomes justify NAT-NATURE.

## 11. Risks

- Topic mismatch between governance/information and direct conservation action.
- No confirmed pilot authority.
- No confirmed scientific partner.
- Insufficient baseline data.
- Overstatement of environmental impact.
- Technology becoming the project objective instead of the enabling mechanism.
- Institutional adoption and interoperability constraints.
