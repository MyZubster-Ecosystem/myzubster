# Pilot Protocol v0.1 — Community Circular Events

**Status:** draft operational protocol for a candidate LIFE-oriented demonstration. Not an authorised field deployment and not a substitute for scientific, legal, safety or environmental review.

## 1. Pilot objective

Test whether a small, authorised cultural/community event or project can measurably improve traceable reuse/recirculation of selected materials using MyZubster as the digital evidence and coordination layer.

The pilot must answer:

1. Can selected materials be registered with sufficient provenance?
2. Can real reuse/repair/recovery actions be evidenced?
3. Can material flows be measured against a pre-defined baseline?
4. Can the same workflow be reproduced by another independent node/community?

## 2. Entry criteria

The pilot cannot start until all mandatory entry criteria are met:

- authorised event/project operator identified;
- explicit pilot participation confirmed;
- environmental/scientific methodology owner identified;
- pilot scope and eligible materials defined;
- baseline method approved;
- measurement units and KPI formulas documented;
- relevant safety/legal requirements reviewed;
- data/privacy responsibilities assigned;
- evidence storage and validation workflow ready;
- no unverified organisation is represented as a partner.

## 3. Pilot roles

Minimum roles:

- **Pilot operator** — responsible for the real-world site/activity.
- **Material custodian** — responsible for material handover/use/recovery records.
- **Environmental/scientific reviewer** — approves baseline/KPI methodology and validates environmental interpretation.
- **Evidence reviewer** — checks completeness and traceability of evidence records.
- **MyZubster technical operator** — maintains registry, evidence workflow and technical logs.
- **Community liaison** — handles consent, stakeholder communication and dissemination.

One person may hold more than one role only where governance and conflict-of-interest rules permit it.

## 4. Pilot scope

Keep the first pilot deliberately small.

Recommended first test:

- 1 authorised event/community project;
- 1 to 3 material categories;
- a bounded list of material items/lots;
- one pre-pilot baseline period;
- one event/use cycle;
- one post-use recovery window;
- one attempted second-use/replication step.

Avoid introducing carbon accounting in v0.1 unless a competent partner provides an accepted methodology. Start with directly measurable physical flows and traceability.

## 5. Baseline

Before materials enter the intervention, record the comparison condition that will be used for evaluation.

Possible baseline questions:

- How were equivalent materials normally sourced?
- What proportion was previously reused/repaired?
- What proportion was discarded or not tracked?
- What evidence existed before MyZubster tracking?
- How many material cycles were documented before the pilot?

Baseline data source, period, limitations and validation owner must be recorded.

If no defensible baseline exists, label the pilot `OBSERVATIONAL` and do not claim improvement.

## 6. Minimum KPI set for v0.1

Subject to methodology approval, start with:

### KPI-01 — Registered material quantity
Physical quantity entering the eligible pilot dataset.

### KPI-02 — Verified reuse quantity
Quantity with evidence of actual use in the authorised pilot.

### KPI-03 — Recovery quantity
Quantity recovered and accounted for after the use cycle.

### KPI-04 — Second-use quantity
Quantity with evidence of entry into an additional documented use cycle.

### KPI-05 — Evidence completeness
Share of counted material/use-cycle records with all mandatory evidence fields present.

### KPI-06 — Provenance completeness
Share of counted material items meeting the agreed provenance requirement.

### KPI-07 — Replication completion
Whether an independent authorised node/site can reproduce the documented workflow with a test dataset or real bounded deployment.

Targets remain `TBD` until baseline and methodology review are complete.

## 7. Pre-pilot procedure

1. Create `PILOT-ID` and freeze scope/version.
2. Register confirmed stakeholders and roles.
3. Record consent/authorisation evidence.
4. Define material categories and eligibility rules.
5. Define baseline and KPI formulas.
6. Register candidate material items/lots.
7. Complete required fitness/safety assessment references.
8. Record entry measurements.
9. Attach provenance evidence.
10. Run evidence completeness check before deployment.

No material enters KPI counting if mandatory eligibility fields are incomplete.

## 8. Deployment procedure

During the authorised event/project:

- link each material item/lot to its `UseCycle`;
- record quantity actually used;
- record repairs/transformations separately;
- capture permitted evidence references;
- log material transfers between custodians;
- keep sensitive locations restricted;
- record exceptions/losses/damage instead of silently removing them from the dataset.

Social posts, photos or Metaverse views may support dissemination but do not substitute for the agreed measurement evidence.

## 9. Post-use recovery procedure

Within the defined recovery window:

1. Reconcile material-out against material-in.
2. Record recovered quantity.
3. Record damaged, missing, disposed or untraceable quantity explicitly.
4. Attach recovery/weight/count evidence.
5. Update condition.
6. Identify next eligible destination.
7. Open a second `UseCycle` only when the next use actually begins.

## 10. Validation

The dataset must pass three distinct checks:

### Technical validation
Schema completeness, IDs, timestamps, references and duplicate detection.

### Evidence validation
Evidence supports the claimed event/measurement and is attributable.

### Environmental interpretation
A competent reviewer confirms whether the measured result supports the environmental claim being made.

These checks must not be collapsed into one automated status.

## 11. Reporting

A public pilot report should include:

- pilot scope and dates;
- responsible roles/organisations that consent to be named;
- baseline and limitations;
- KPI definitions;
- measured values;
- validation status;
- missing/failed records;
- safety/privacy boundaries;
- replication outcome;
- links to public evidence where appropriate;
- explicit statement of what the pilot does **not** prove.

Do not report unvalidated values as final impact.

## 12. Social / community engagement protocol

Social channels are used to discover and inform potential stakeholders.

Allowed sequence:

`public post -> inbound interest -> direct contact -> consent -> role review -> confirmed pilot participation`

Do not treat likes, comments, group membership or cultural association as pilot consent.

If a free-party/underground community wishes to participate, document the specific participating organiser/entity and role rather than attributing participation to an entire scene or historical collective.

## 13. Infopoint / Metaverse use

The pilot may expose a public digital layer containing:

- non-sensitive material stories/passports;
- workshops and reuse instructions;
- validated KPI summaries;
- public replication documentation;
- links to the Circular Marketplace;
- project updates.

No private event location or personal information should be made public by default.

## 14. Replication test

After the first validated run:

1. Freeze toolkit version.
2. Publish configuration/schema/documentation.
3. Provide Docker/local deployment instructions for the required technical components.
4. Provide a synthetic test dataset if real data cannot be shared.
5. Ask an independent authorised node/community to reproduce the workflow.
6. Record setup time, blockers, deviations and outcome.
7. Update the replication package before any scale claim.

## 15. Stop conditions

Pause or stop the pilot if:

- authorisation is withdrawn;
- required safety conditions are not met;
- evidence integrity is compromised;
- material provenance is materially unreliable;
- privacy/sensitive location exposure occurs;
- environmental methodology cannot support the intended claim;
- an organisation/community is being represented beyond its confirmed role.

## 16. Exit criteria for v0.1

The pilot is considered operationally complete only when:

- scope and baseline are frozen;
- materials are registered;
- at least one real authorised use cycle is completed;
- post-use recovery is reconciled;
- KPI records are generated;
- validation states are recorded;
- a public-safe report is produced;
- replication package is prepared;
- at least one independent replication attempt is documented.

Operational completion does not mean LIFE approval or proven large-scale environmental impact.
