# MyZubster — Community Circular Events Candidate Pilot

**Status:** candidate pilot / working concept. Not a confirmed LIFE project, beneficiary, partnership or authorised field deployment.

## Purpose

This document defines a possible LIFE-oriented demonstration track connecting MyZubster's circular-economy infrastructure with cultural/community events, open-source replication and stakeholder engagement.

The environmental objective is the core of the pilot. Free-party, DIY, maker and underground communities are considered possible demonstration and engagement environments, not environmental outcomes by themselves.

No affiliation, endorsement or partnership with Spiral Tribe/SP23 or any other cultural collective is claimed unless independently and formally confirmed.

## Environmental hypothesis

Temporary cultural and community events can consume significant quantities of reusable materials and equipment. A traceable circular workflow could help recover, catalogue, repair, exchange, reuse and recirculate suitable materials across multiple events or community projects.

Proposed chain:

`material/product -> baseline/provenance -> Circular Marketplace -> authorised pilot event/project -> reuse/repair/transformation -> post-use recovery -> next use -> evidence -> KPI -> replication`

## MyZubster role

MyZubster would be the enabling digital infrastructure, not the environmental objective itself. Candidate functions include:

- material and product registry;
- provenance and lifecycle records;
- Circular Marketplace discovery/exchange;
- evidence records and validation states;
- event/project association;
- public Infopoint for authorised information;
- Metaverse visualisation/education layer;
- social and messaging dissemination;
- GitHub-based open documentation;
- Docker-based replication package for independent/community nodes;
- Zorgax-assisted classification, preparation and coordination under human governance.

## Candidate pilot workflow

1. Define an authorised real-world event/community pilot and responsible operator.
2. Establish a baseline before intervention.
3. Catalogue eligible recovered/reusable materials and equipment.
4. Record provenance, condition, intended use and safety/fitness information where relevant.
5. Make eligible resources discoverable through the Circular Marketplace.
6. Associate selected materials with the pilot event/project.
7. Record reuse, repair or transformation actions.
8. Recover and account for materials after use.
9. Record subsequent destination/reuse where possible.
10. Validate evidence and calculate agreed KPIs.
11. Publish non-sensitive results through MyZubster/Infopoint/Metaverse/social channels.
12. Package the technical and operational method for replication.

## Stakeholder engagement through social channels

Social communication can support stakeholder discovery and recruitment, but social engagement is not evidence of partnership or environmental impact.

Proposed funnel:

`social communication -> expressed interest -> consent/contact -> eligibility check -> pilot role -> documented activity -> evidence`

Potential stakeholder categories include:

- event and cultural organisers;
- maker and repair communities;
- artists and fabricators;
- reuse/recycling operators;
- municipalities or venue operators;
- environmental/scientific partners;
- community contributors;
- replication sites.

Interest, comments, likes or shares must not be represented as formal participation.

## Infopoint and Metaverse

The Infopoint and Metaverse can provide dissemination and education around the environmental workflow: material origin, reuse instructions, project documentation, workshops, public KPIs and replication resources.

They must not be treated as environmental evidence by themselves. Environmental claims must come from validated field data and an agreed methodology.

Sensitive/private event locations and personal data must not be exposed automatically.

## GitHub and Docker replication

GitHub can provide transparent versioning of software, documentation, schemas and replication instructions. Docker can support reproducible deployment of the necessary technical services on an independent PC/server or community node.

Candidate replication path:

`repository -> documented configuration -> Docker deployment -> local node -> test dataset -> validated workflow -> optional interoperability with wider MyZubster infrastructure`

A successful replication requires more than cloning code: the operational method, governance, data requirements, environmental methodology and validation rules must also be transferable.

## Governance

Zorgax/AI may assist with classification, documentation, reminders, data preparation and coordination. Human authorisation remains required for consequential actions including publication of sensitive information, identity claims, partnerships, field deployment, financial commitments and environmental claims.

## Relationship to the current LIFE concept

The current MyZubster LIFE 2026 concept note focuses on biodiversity/protected-area evidence and monitoring and explicitly states that the technology is an enabling base, while environmental objectives, scientific baseline, measurable outcomes and a real pilot remain central.

This community circular-events concept is therefore maintained as a separate candidate demonstration track until topic fit is assessed. It must not silently replace or broaden the current biodiversity concept note.

Before inclusion in any LIFE application, the team should confirm:

- appropriate LIFE topic/call fit with the NCP;
- eligible environmental objective and intervention logic;
- competent pilot operator/authority;
- scientific/environmental methodology partner;
- baseline and KPI methodology;
- legal/safety requirements for real-world events and material reuse;
- consortium roles and formal commitments;
- complementarity with existing projects;
- budget, work packages and After-LIFE sustainability/replication plan.

## Candidate work-package mapping

- **WP1 — Governance and quality:** permissions, roles, risk, data governance and audit trail.
- **WP2 — Baseline and circular methodology:** material-flow baseline, eligibility, measurement and KPI protocol.
- **WP3 — Digital demonstrator:** registry, provenance, Marketplace, evidence and dashboards/Infopoint.
- **WP4 — Real-world demonstration:** authorised community/event deployment, recovery and monitoring.
- **WP5 — Impact and replication:** validated results, Docker/open-source replication package and adoption pathway.
- **WP6 — Communication and dissemination:** stakeholder engagement, Metaverse/Infopoint interpretation and public results.

## Immediate evidence-building actions

- Create a stakeholder-interest register with explicit consent/status fields.
- Identify one legally authorised demonstration event/community project.
- Identify a reuse/recycling/material partner.
- Identify an environmental/scientific methodology partner.
- Define the minimum material passport/evidence schema.
- Select a small set of measurable material flows.
- Run a pre-pilot baseline.
- Test post-event recovery and second-use tracking.
- Document every claim with source/evidence status.
- Keep cultural references separate from formal partnership claims.
